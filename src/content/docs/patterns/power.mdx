KDE apps are known for how much they let people do and change. That reputation is worth keeping, and it is also
easy to turn into clutter. The idea is to offer power without charging everyone for it: the simple path stays
simple (see [Simple by default](/patterns/first-launch/)), and the depth is one deliberate step away.

## Settings

A setting exists so that people who work differently can each work their way.

<DoDont>
  <Do sample="Clicking files:  ◉ Opens them  ○ Selects them">
    Offer a choice between behaviours that are each right for someone.
  </Do>
  <Dont sample="☐ Enable thumbnails">
    Add a switch that merely turns a feature off. It is usually a sign that the feature is not finished.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

Do not add a setting:

- **to avoid making a decision.** Choosing a good default is the designer's job.
- **to work around a bug.** Fix the bug.
- **to override the system.** Colours, fonts, animation speed and the like belong to the desktop's settings;
  follow them.
- **because “KDE is about options”.** Every setting is something to read, understand and test. Too many make an
  app harder for everyone.

Appearance options are the least valuable kind. Do not weaken the default look to make room for them.

Build settings pages with a [form layout](/components/form-layout/), and apply changes immediately, without
asking for a restart.

## Do not call it “Advanced”

A page or section named “Advanced” says nothing about what is in it. Beginners are scared off, experts have to
open it to find out.

Use **progressive disclosure** instead: show the simple version first, and let people open the detailed one from
it. System Settings does this for colours: the page shows a grid of colour schemes to pick from, and each scheme
has an Edit button that opens the full editor in its own window.

| Instead of… | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| An “Advanced” tab | Name the tab after what it contains |
| A long page with rare options at the bottom | Move them to a sub-page reached from a clearly named row |
| A dialog full of expert fields | A simple dialog with a “Details” section that expands |

## Accelerators

Keyboard shortcuts, context menus and gestures let practised users go faster. They are extra routes, never the
only one.

<Example
  name="components/action-display-hints"

  caption="Every action here is visible or one click away. Shortcuts for them are a bonus on top."
  code={false}

- **Give every action a shortcut**, or at least let users assign one. Use the standard shortcut for standard
  actions.
- **Do not use the Meta key**; it is reserved for the desktop.
- **Use only two-finger gestures.** Three- and four-finger gestures belong to the system.
- **Anything in a context menu must also be reachable another way.**

See [Actions and toolbars](/components/actions/) for how shortcuts are attached to actions.

## Extensions

If your app can be extended with plugins, themes or other downloadable content, use KDE's KNewStuff framework to
fetch it. If you build your own downloader instead, warn people clearly that third-party content comes with no
promise of quality or safety.

## Who you are designing for

KDE's guidelines are frank about the audience: people with at least basic computer knowledge, up to experts and
professionals. They are assumed to know what windows, buttons, menus, lists and tabs are. You are allowed to
choose your users, and you do not have to design for someone who has never used a computer. What you may not do
is make the common task hard for the sake of the rare one.
