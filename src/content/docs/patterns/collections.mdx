## List, grid or table?

| The items are… | Use | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mostly text: names, messages, settings | A **list** | `ListView` and [list items](/components/lists/) |
| Mostly visual: photos, covers, apps | A **grid**, or [cards](/components/cards/) | `GridView`, `Kirigami.CardsLayout` |
| Records with three or more fields worth comparing | A **table** | `TableView` |

If you are tempted to make a table with two columns, make a list with a title and a subtitle instead.

### Avoid trees

Tree views, with rows that expand into indented child rows, are hard to use with touch and easy to get lost in.
Prefer a list with section headers, a list that pushes a new page, or collapsible sections.

## Making lists easy to scan

- **Make the selected or active item stand out.** The highlight colour marks the current row; bold text marks
  the item that is in use, such as the connected network or the playing track.
- **Vary the icons, or leave them out.** A column of identical icons tells nobody anything.
- **Striped rows** help the eye in wide lists and tables. Turn them on with
  `Kirigami.Theme.useAlternateBackgroundColor: true` on the view; do not paint them yourself.
- **Group long lists** under section headers.

## Adding and removing items

<Example
  name="components/list-view-header"
  alt="A list of three accounts under a header bar reading Accounts, with an Add Account button at its right end"
  caption="The button that adds to this list lives in the list’s own header."
/>

- **Controls that add to a list belong to the list.** Put them in a `Kirigami.InlineViewHeader` at the top of
  the view, not far away in the window's toolbar.
- **Controls that act on one row go on that row**, at its trailing end, as icon-only buttons. Keep them always
  visible; a button that appears only on hover does not exist for touch users. Use at most two per row, and
  preferably none or one.
- **Removing should be easy to undo.** See [Feedback and status](/patterns/feedback/).

## Tabs

There are two kinds of tabs and they should not be confused.

| Kind | What the tabs are | Component | Placement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Navigation tabs** | The fixed places of your app | `Kirigami.NavigationTabBar` | Above content on desktop, below on phones |
| **Document tabs** | Things the user opened: web pages, files, chats | `QQC2.TabBar` | Above the content, full width |

Document tabs can be closed and reordered by the user, so each needs a close button. Hide the tab bar when only
one tab is open.

Tabs do not scale. As a way to split one view into sections they stop working beyond four or five tabs on a
desktop, and two on a phone. If you have more, use a list that leads to pages.

## Showing extra detail

| The detail is… | Show it… |
| --- | --- |
| Large enough to fill the window | On a new [page](/components/page/) |
| Read-only and possibly long | In an [overlay sheet](/components/overlay-sheet/) |
| A question or a small form | In a [dialog](/components/dialogs/) |

Anything that floats over the content needs a visible edge, so it does not melt into the background in dark
colour schemes. Kirigami's own sheets, dialogs and cards have one.
