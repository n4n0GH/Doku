## Where things go

People look at the top-leading corner of a window first (top left in English). Put the most important thing
there. On a phone, the easiest place to reach is the bottom, so that is where controls go.

The guidelines give each part of an app a home, and a different one when space is short:

| Part | Wide window | Narrow window or phone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Toolbar | Above the content | Below the content |
| Sidebar | At the leading edge | The first page of the stack, or opened on demand |
| Preview or tool panel | At the trailing edge | Opened on demand |
| Navigation tab bar | Above the content | Below the content |
| Menu bar | Above the toolbar | A menu button on the toolbar |
| Status bar | Below the content | Left out |

If a view needs its own controls, such as a zoom slider for a grid, put them in a second toolbar directly under
the main one.

## Choosing how people get around

Pick by the shape of your app, not by taste.

| Your app has… | Use | See |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One flow that goes deeper step by step | The page stack: `pageStack.push()` | [Pages](/components/page/) |
| Up to five top-level places | `Kirigami.NavigationTabBar` | [Navigation tab bar](/components/navigation-tab-bar/) |
| More than five top-level places | A `Kirigami.GlobalDrawer` sidebar | [Drawers](/components/drawers/) |

Whichever you choose, the view people see at launch should be the first item.

Kirigami does much of this for you, but only if you let it:

- **Build from pages, actions and drawers**, not from fixed-position items. They are the parts that know how to
  move.
- **Do not shrink a desktop layout onto a phone.** A phone needs fewer things on screen at once and bigger touch
  targets. Check `Kirigami.Settings.isMobile` when you need to make a different choice, and
  `Kirigami.Settings.tabletMode` for touch-first use on larger screens.
- **Try it.** Start your app with `QT_QUICK_CONTROLS_MOBILE=1` to see the phone arrangement on your desktop, and
  drag the window narrow to see the columns collapse.

## Menus

A menu button (☰) is fine while the menu has about 15 items or fewer. Beyond that, a real menu bar is easier to
search by eye. Do not put Quit or Minimize in a menu button; the window already has those.

## Keyboard, touchpad and touch

- **Every action should be reachable from the keyboard.** Give standard actions their standard shortcut and let
  people reassign shortcuts where you can.
- **Shortcuts use a modifier plus a letter or number**, never a function key alone; laptop keyboards often hide
  those behind another key. Leave the Meta key alone; it belongs to the desktop.
- **Shortcuts, context menus and gestures are accelerators.** They make experts faster, but nothing important may
  be reachable *only* that way. The main route is always something visible.
- **On touchpads and touchscreens, use two-finger gestures only.** Three and four fingers are reserved for the
  system. Scrolling with a touchpad should move content smoothly, pixel by pixel.

## Right-to-left languages

In Arabic, Hebrew and other right-to-left languages the whole layout is mirrored: sidebars move to the right, back
arrows point the other way. That is why this guide says “leading” and “trailing” instead of left and right. Kirigami
components and Qt Quick layouts mirror by themselves; images are not mirrored. Test with `LANGUAGE=ar_AR`.
